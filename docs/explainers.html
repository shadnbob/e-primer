Explainer Playground

Contested terms, explained in place

Explainer dictionaries annotate terms whose meanings shift between countries, eras, disciplines, and speakers — with context, not accusations. This page exists to exercise them: with the extension installed, the passages below should light up, and the guard passages should stay clean. Back to the main page or the full pattern reference.

How to use this page
  1. Load the extension (Chrome: chrome://extensions → Load unpacked → dist/).
  2. Open this page. Highlights appear within a second or two.
  3. Click any highlight — explainer cards carry a gray Context badge, never a severity level.
  4. Check the dashed guard passages: nothing in them should be highlighted by the explainers.

Political Spectrum Labels

🧭 Left / Right 📜 Liberal 🏛️ Conservative

Direction-words inherited from 1789 seating charts, and labels that reversed meaning as they crossed oceans. The cards explain where each label came from and why two readers can take opposite meanings from the same sentence.

Should highlight

Left-wing commentators accused the government of drifting toward the far right, while conservatives insisted the reforms were simply prudent. One columnist described the senator as liberal on trade but pointed out that the left has never fully trusted him; rightist papers, meanwhile, celebrated what they called a victory for the center-right.

Expected: left-wing, far right, conservatives, liberal, the left, rightist, center-right — each with its family's card.
Should stay clean

You have the right to remain silent. A conservative estimate puts the repair bill at five thousand dollars, which left the committee with little choice: turn left at the second junction, apply a liberal amount of primer, and hope the liberal arts faculty approves the budget at the right time.

Everyday senses only — no spectrum highlights.

Science & Statistics

🔬 Proof & "The Science" 📊 Statistical Significance 🔗 Linked & Associated ⚖️ Relative vs Absolute Risk 🔍 No Evidence 🌿 Natural & Chemical-Free

Research and marketing phrases whose technical meaning differs from how they read. The cards hand you the question to ask: What's the effect size? What's the baseline? Who looked, and how hard?

Should highlight — health news

A new study found that daily coffee is linked to a significantly lower risk of stroke: regular drinkers were 30% less likely to be affected, and researchers reported a significant reduction in related hospital visits. Skeptics note the drink merely correlates with wealth — but supporters say the association between coffee and health keeps showing up, and that critics dismiss it as just a theory.

Expected: linked to, significantly lower, 30% less likely, significant reduction, correlates with(–ish), association between, just a theory.
Should highlight — marketing copy

Our chemical-free detox tea is scientifically proven to flush out toxins — an all-natural superfood blend with no evidence of side effects. Clinically proven, trusted by thousands: this doubles your chances of feeling great, because the science says so.

Expected: chemical-free, detox, scientifically proven, toxins, all-natural, superfood, no evidence, clinically proven, doubles your chances, the science says.
Should stay clean

She brought her significant other to the lecture on music theory, having made a significant investment in tickets. In theory the professor would discuss the toxin isolated from the bacterium, but he spent the hour on game theory instead.

Everyday and technical-singular senses only — no science & statistics highlights.

Political -isms

🏭 Socialism 🏦 Capitalism 📚 Fascism 📣 Populism 🗺️ Nationalism & Globalism

System-words whose referents differ so much between speakers that arguments about them are often arguments about different things. The cards anchor each word to its history and ask which variant is actually meant.

Should highlight

The mayor's opponents branded the transit plan socialism, while her allies said the city was finally taming crony capitalism. A populist challenger blamed globalist trade deals; a rival called that rhetoric a slide toward fascism. Editorials asked whether nationalist industrial policy could coexist with the free market, and whether democratic socialists and social democrats even want the same thing.

Expected: socialism, crony capitalism, populist, globalist, fascism, nationalist, free market, democratic socialists, social democrats.
Should stay clean

The venture capitalist wired the funds on Tuesday. Venture capitalists rarely attend municipal budget hearings, she noted, and the social committee's optimism about the gala remained unshaken.

"Venture capitalist(s)" is a job title, and "social" alone is not "socialism" — no -ism highlights.

Speech & Civic Terms

🗣️ Free Speech ✂️ Censorship 📜 Rights Claims ⚖️ Legal Standards

The most common online argument-collapse: legal standards and civic values whose legal sense and cultural sense get swapped mid-argument. The cards ask who exercised what power, and whether the standard invoked binds the actor in question.

Should highlight

After the video was removed, its creator said the platform had committed censorship and violated his free speech; commenters replied that the First Amendment binds governments, not websites. "I have a right to be heard," he insisted, calling the takedown defamatory and demanding due process. Meanwhile a columnist who was deplatformed last year claimed a shadow ban, and both invoked freedom of expression.

Expected: censorship, free speech, First Amendment, I have a right to, defamatory, due process, deplatformed, shadow ban, freedom of expression.
Should stay clean

The banned substances list grew again this season, and animal rights groups objected to the venue. During the procession the priest swung the censer, and the choir was silenced only by the organ's final chord.

"Banned", "animal rights", "censer", and "silenced" are outside the dictionary — no civic-term highlights.

Economic Terms

📈 Inflation Is a Rate 🏛️ Deficit vs Debt 🌡️ Recession & "The Economy" 🏠 Middle Class & Records

The vocabulary news cycles run on, where a rate reads as a level, a flow as a stock, and one measure as "the economy." The cards hand over the distinction and the numbers to ask for.

Should highlight

Officials celebrated that inflation is cooling, though the national debt kept climbing even as Congress claimed credit for cutting the deficit. Analysts argued about whether a recession had already begun and whether the new tariffs were good for the economy; the same week brought record profits for retailers and another promise of relief for the middle class.

Expected: inflation is cooling, national debt, cutting the deficit, recession, good for the economy, record profits, middle class.
Should stay clean

Down eight points, the team erased the deficit in the fourth quarter while record high temperatures baked the stadium. Afterward the mascot deflated the balloon arch, which the crew judged a soft landing for an inflatable of that size.

Sports deficits, weather records, balloons, and aviation metaphors — no economic-term highlights.

Media & Truth Terms

📰 Fake News 🔀 Mis- & Disinformation 🧵 Conspiracy Theory 📡 Narratives & "The Media"

The meta-layer: the words we use to talk about truth and coverage itself. Each one both describes a real phenomenon and gets used to dismiss opponents — the cards present both faces and the question that separates them.

Should highlight

The senator dismissed the report as fake news from the mainstream media, while fact-checkers labeled his response misinformation — possibly disinformation, one argued. His supporters urged followers to do your own research rather than trust legacy media pushing a narrative; critics answered that the whole affair had become an echo chamber feeding a conspiracy theory.

Expected: fake news, mainstream media, fact-checkers, misinformation, disinformation, do your own research, legacy media, pushing a narrative, echo chamber, conspiracy theory.
Should stay clean

The professor praised the narrative structure of the novel, then turned to the statute: conspiracy to commit fraud carries heavy penalties. On the way out, the media player in the lobby crashed again.

Literary narratives, criminal conspiracy, and media players — no media-term highlights.
